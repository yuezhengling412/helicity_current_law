Based on Chandran, B. D. G., Mallet, A., & Meyrand, R. (2026). *The Second Quadratic Invariant of Astrophysical Gyrokinetics*. [arXiv:2607.27981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27981).

Consider a uniform background magnetic field

$$\boldsymbol B_0=B_0\hat{\boldsymbol z}$$

and the gyrokinetic ordering

$$\frac{k_\parallel}{k_\perp}\sim\frac{\omega}{\Omega_i}\sim\epsilon_{\rm GK}\ll1.$$

Assume uniform background density and temperature, periodic boundary conditions in the perpendicular directions, and neglect external forcing and collisions. Gaussian units are used, with $T_s$ expressed in energy units.

Define the nonadiabatic distribution-function perturbation $h_s$, the equilibrium distribution $F_{0s}$, and the gyroaveraged generalized potential

$$\bar\chi_s=\left\langle\phi-\frac{\boldsymbol v\cdot\boldsymbol A}{c}\right\rangle_{\boldsymbol R_s}.$$

The gyrokinetic equation is

$$\frac{\partial h_s}{\partial t}+v_\parallel\frac{\partial h_s}{\partial z}+\frac{c}{B_0}\{\bar\chi_s,h_s\}=\frac{q_sF_{0s}}{T_s}\frac{\partial\bar\chi_s}{\partial t},\tag{1}$$

where

$$\{a,b\}=\hat{\boldsymbol z}\cdot(\nabla_\perp a\times\nabla_\perp b).$$

Introduce

$$\psi_s=h_s-\frac{q_sF_{0s}}{T_s}\bar\chi_s.\tag{2}$$

Since

$$\{\bar\chi_s,\bar\chi_s\}=0,$$

Equation (1) becomes

$$\frac{\partial\psi_s}{\partial t}+v_\parallel\frac{\partial h_s}{\partial z}+\frac{c}{B_0}\{\bar\chi_s,\psi_s\}=0.\tag{3}$$

For the second quadratic invariant defined by Chandran, Mallet, and Meyrand (2026), the signed real part is

$$\mathcal H_R=\int dz\,\mathfrak h(z,t),$$

where

$$\mathfrak h(z,t)=\sum_s\left\langle\operatorname{PV}\!\int\frac{T_s\psi_s^2}{2v_\parallel F_{0s}}\,d^3v\right\rangle_\perp.\tag{4}$$

$\operatorname{PV}$ denotes the Cauchy principal value, and $\langle\cdots\rangle_\perp$ denotes averaging over the perpendicular cross-section.

Parallel helicity flux

Multiply equation (3) by

$$\frac{T_s\psi_s}{v_\parallel F_{0s}},$$

Integrate over velocity, average over the perpendicular cross-section, and sum over species.

The periodic boundary conditions give

$$\left\langle\{\bar\chi_s,\psi_s^2\}\right\rangle_\perp=0.$$

Therefore

$$\frac{\partial\mathfrak h}{\partial t}=-\sum_s\left\langle\int\frac{T_s\psi_s}{F_{0s}}\frac{\partial h_s}{\partial z}\,d^3v\right\rangle_\perp.\tag{5}$$

Substituting equation (2):

$$\frac{\partial\mathfrak h}{\partial t}=-\sum_s\left\langle\int\frac{T_sh_s}{F_{0s}}\frac{\partial h_s}{\partial z}\,d^3v\right\rangle_\perp+\sum_s\left\langle\int q_s\bar\chi_s\frac{\partial h_s}{\partial z}\,d^3v\right\rangle_\perp.\tag{6}$$

Define

$$K_h=\sum_s\left\langle\int\frac{T_sh_s^2}{2F_{0s}}\,d^3v\right\rangle_\perp,\tag{7}$$

$$I=\sum_s\left\langle\int q_s\bar\chi_sh_s\,d^3v\right\rangle_\perp,\tag{8}$$

$$J=\sum_s\left\langle\int q_sh_s\frac{\partial\bar\chi_s}{\partial z}\,d^3v\right\rangle_\perp.\tag{9}$$

Thus

$$\frac{\partial\mathfrak h}{\partial t}=-\frac{\partial K_h}{\partial z}+\frac{\partial I}{\partial z}-J.\tag{10}$$

Elimination of the field–particle cross terms

Define

$$C=\sum_s\frac{q_s^2n_{0s}}{T_s}.\tag{11}$$

The quasineutrality condition is

$$\sum_s q_s\int\langle h_s\rangle_{\boldsymbol r}\,d^3v=C\phi.\tag{12}$$

Using the Coulomb gauge

$$\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol A=0,$$

the corresponding Ampère’s law is

$$\sum_s q_s\int\boldsymbol v\langle h_s\rangle_{\boldsymbol r}\,d^3v=-\frac{c}{4\pi}\nabla_\perp^2\boldsymbol A.\tag{13}$$

Expressing equation (8) as an integral over particle position:

$$I=\left\langle\phi\sum_s q_s\int\langle h_s\rangle_{\boldsymbol r}\,d^3v\right\rangle_\perp-\frac1c\left\langle\boldsymbol A\cdot\sum_s q_s\int\boldsymbol v\langle h_s\rangle_{\boldsymbol r}\,d^3v\right\rangle_\perp.\tag{14}$$

Using equations (12) and (13):

$$I=C\langle\phi^2\rangle_\perp+\frac1{4\pi}\left\langle\boldsymbol A\cdot\nabla_\perp^2\boldsymbol A\right\rangle_\perp.\tag{15}$$

Integration by parts in the perpendicular directions gives

$$\left\langle\boldsymbol A\cdot\nabla_\perp^2\boldsymbol A\right\rangle_\perp=-\left\langle|\nabla_\perp\boldsymbol A|^2\right\rangle_\perp.$$

Define

$$K_B=\frac{\left\langle|\nabla_\perp\boldsymbol A|^2\right\rangle_\perp}{8\pi},\tag{16}$$

giving

$$I=C\langle\phi^2\rangle_\perp-2K_B.\tag{17}$$

Similarly,

$$J=C\left\langle\phi\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial z}\right\rangle_\perp+\frac1{4\pi}\left\langle\frac{\partial\boldsymbol A}{\partial z}\cdot\nabla_\perp^2\boldsymbol A\right\rangle_\perp.\tag{18}$$

Since $C$ is independent of $z$,

$$J=\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\left[\frac C2\langle\phi^2\rangle_\perp-K_B\right].\tag{19}$$

Substituting equations (17) and (19) into equation (10):

$$\frac{\partial\mathfrak h}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\left[K_h-\frac C2\langle\phi^2\rangle_\perp+K_B\right]=0.\tag{20}$$

The parallel helicity flux is therefore

$$J_{\mathcal H,\parallel}=K_h-\frac C2\langle\phi^2\rangle_\perp+K_B.\tag{21}$$

Equality of the helicity flux and the free-energy density

The total gyrokinetic free-energy density is defined as

$$W=\left\langle\frac{|\delta\boldsymbol B|^2}{8\pi}+\sum_s\int\frac{T_s(\delta f_s)^2}{2F_{0s}}\,d^3v\right\rangle_\perp.\tag{22}$$

where

$$\delta f_s=h_s-\frac{q_sF_{0s}}{T_s}\phi,\tag{23}$$

Expanding the particle free energy:

$$W_{\rm p}=\sum_s\left\langle\int\frac{T_s(\delta f_s)^2}{2F_{0s}}\,d^3v\right\rangle_\perp=K_h-\left\langle\phi\sum_s q_s\int\langle h_s\rangle_{\boldsymbol r}\,d^3v\right\rangle_\perp+\frac12\sum_s\frac{q_s^2n_{0s}}{T_s}\langle\phi^2\rangle_\perp.\tag{24}$$

Using the quasineutrality condition:

$$W_{\rm p}=K_h-\frac C2\langle\phi^2\rangle_\perp.\tag{25}$$

At the retained order in gyrokinetics,

$$\left\langle\frac{|\delta\boldsymbol B|^2}{8\pi}\right\rangle_\perp=K_B.\tag{26}$$

Therefore

$$W=K_h-\frac C2\langle\phi^2\rangle_\perp+K_B.\tag{27}$$

Comparing equations (21) and (27) gives

$$J_{\mathcal H,\parallel}=W.\tag{28}$$

Equation (20) then becomes

$$\frac{\partial\mathfrak h}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial W}{\partial z}=0.\tag{29}$$

Since the free energy is positive definite,

$$J_{\mathcal H,\parallel}=W=\left\langle\frac{|\delta\boldsymbol B|^2}{8\pi}+\sum_s\int\frac{T_s(\delta f_s)^2}{2F_{0s}}\,d^3v\right\rangle_\perp\geq0.\tag{30}$$

That is, the parallel flux of total gyrokinetic helicity = the total fluctuation free-energy density

Helicity source–sink separation and the stored free energy

Including helicity source and sink terms:

$$\frac{\partial\mathfrak h}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial W}{\partial z}=S_{\mathcal H}-D_{\mathcal H}.\tag{31}$$

For a statistically stationary state,

$$\frac{dW}{dz}=S_{\mathcal H}-D_{\mathcal H}.\tag{32}$$

Assuming $W\to0$ outside the source–sink region, multiply equation (32) by $z$ and integrate:

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}W\,dz=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}z(D_{\mathcal H}-S_{\mathcal H})\,dz.\tag{33}$$

For a system with constant cross-sectional area $A$, suppose positive helicity is injected at $z_i$ and removed at $z_d$ at a rate $\dot{\mathcal H}_0$. Then

$$\mathscr W\equiv A\int W\,dz=\dot{\mathcal H}_0(z_d-z_i).\tag{34}$$

Here $\mathscr W$ is the total fluctuation free energy in the system. For distributed sources and sinks of the same sign, $z_i,z_d$ are their respective flux-weighted centroids.
